Privacy Policy

Effective: October 8, 2026

I Hereby Commit is a project of Hatch Labs Holdings LLC, a Texas limited liability company ("Hatch Labs," "we," "us"). This policy explains what we collect when you apply at iherebycommit.com, what we receive if you later choose to connect another account (such as Google, Facebook, Oura, Apple Health in our iPhone app, or a bank account through Plaid), how we use it, and the choices you have.

The short version

  • Applying isn't joining the study. Before the study begins, you'll get full details and a separate consent form, and your individual answers become study data only if you agree then.
  • We may publish aggregate statistics about who applied — never anything that identifies you.
  • We never sell your information, and we don't use it for third-party advertising.
  • We may email you about other apps and products built by Hatch Labs. You can unsubscribe anytime.
  • Scientists only ever receive de-identified, anonymized data — never your name or contact details.
  • Where we offer it, connecting Google, Facebook, Oura, Apple Health, or a bank account is always optional and comes after you apply. Each has its own consent step, and you can disconnect anytime.
  • Data from connected accounts is never sold, never used for ads or marketing, and never used to train AI. Google contacts and Facebook friends are never used for research, not even as totals.
  • You can ask us to delete your information at any time. See how to delete your data.

What we collect

From you, when you apply: your name; date of birth; gender; height; your city or ZIP/postal code, and, if you choose to share it, your location on a map (we store it rounded to about 1 km); whether you're applying as a single or as a couple; if you apply as a couple, your relationship stage and your partner's name, email, and the other details you give about them; whether you have or want children and related family plans; optional details such as your ethnicity, religion, politics, education, and income range; what you're looking for in a partner and what matters most to you; your email address; your mobile number if you give us one; and the social profile links you choose to share (LinkedIn, Instagram, X, and TikTok, all optional).

Some of these answers, such as ethnicity, religion, politics, and family plans, are sensitive. They're optional unless the form says otherwise, and we use them only as this policy describes. We save your answers as you fill in the form, so what you've entered may be stored even if you don't finish and submit.

Automatically: your browser type, the page that referred you to us, which headline you were viewing when you applied, and a one-way scrambled ("hashed") version of your IP address that we use only to block spam. We don't currently use analytics, advertising cookies, or third-party tracking pixels. If that changes, we'll update this policy first.

Only if you choose to connect another account: the data described under Connected accounts.

  • To review applications and contact you about Cohort 01 and the study.
  • To select and balance Cohort 01.
  • To send you text messages, only if you opted in: verification codes, pilot study notifications, insights, match and introduction updates (for singles), account alerts, and study check-in reminders.

  • To protect the site from bots and abuse.

Mutual friends and blocking

These features may not be available to everyone yet; this section explains how they work when they are.

One-way codes, not contact lists. When you connect Google contacts or Facebook, or when you block someone, we turn each email address, phone number, or account ID into a one-way keyed code (a "hash") before it's stored. We don't store the names or raw contact details of people in your contacts, and the secret key used to make the codes is kept separately from the data. We never contact anyone because they appear in your contacts.

What other members can see. We can show you study members that you and another member are both connected to. A connection that comes from your contacts counts only for you: we never show anyone your contact list, and we never identify someone who isn't a member to anyone. Because mutual friends work both ways, another member may see that the two of you are connected to the same member.

Blocking. Where blocking is offered, you can block someone, such as an ex, by entering their phone number or email. We store only a one-way code of it. If that person applies, now or later, we use the block to keep the two of you apart in the study's features, such as mutual friends. We may count blocks in de-identified totals, but never who blocked whom.

If you're not a member. If a member connected their contacts or blocked you, we may hold a one-way code of your email or phone number, never your name, linked to that member. We don't contact you because of it. If you later apply with that email or phone number, your application takes it over, so an existing block stays in place.

Google user dataOPTIONAL

What we access. If you tap "Find mutual friends" and connect Google, we ask Google for these permissions: your Google Contacts (read-only, contacts.readonly), your Google "Other contacts" (read-only, contacts.other.readonly), and your basic sign-in info (openid and email). From your contacts and other contacts we read only email addresses and phone numbers. We don't read names, photos, notes, or any other contact fields, and we don't access your Gmail messages, calendar, or files. From your sign-in info we use your Google account ID and, if it matches the email on your application, your verified email address.

Why. Only to show you mutual friends in the study, as described above. Both contacts permissions are needed because "Other contacts" holds people you've emailed but never saved.

How it's stored. Each email address and phone number is converted into a one-way keyed code before it's saved; the raw values are never written to our database or logs. Your Google account ID is stored the same way. It's a one-time, read-only import: we revoke Google's access token right after the import, and re-syncing asks for your permission again.

What we never do. We never sell Google user data. We never use or transfer it for advertising, including retargeting or personalized ads. We never use it to determine credit-worthiness or for lending. We never use it to train, fine-tune, or improve artificial-intelligence or machine-learning models. We never use it for research, analytics, or publishing — not the raw data, and not data aggregated, anonymized, or derived from it. No one at Hatch Labs reads it, except where the law requires, for security purposes such as investigating abuse, or with your express permission. We don't transfer it to anyone except the service providers that host and run this feature for us (Supabase and Cloudflare), only as needed to provide it.

How long we keep it. Until you disconnect Google, delete your application, or 365 days pass without you importing again, whichever comes first.

How to revoke access and delete it. Use Disconnect next to Google on our site, or email us; either way we delete the contact codes, your Google account ID, and the connections created from your import (see deletion steps). You can also review or remove our access at any time in your Google Account at myaccount.google.com/connections. Because we revoke our access right after each import, removing it there doesn't by itself delete what we already imported — use Disconnect or email us for that.

I Hereby Commit's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Oura Ring dataOPTIONAL

This section applies only if you choose to connect an Oura Ring account. Oura data is health data, so we treat it with extra care.

What we collect. We request only the data types listed on our consent screen, and you can switch individual types on or off on Oura's own permission screen. We may request:

  • Daily summaries (Oura "daily" permission): sleep (duration, timing, stages, efficiency, and sleep score), readiness, and activity summaries.
  • Heart rate (Oura "heartrate" permission): heart-rate readings over time.
  • Personal information (Oura "personal" permission): age, biological sex, height, and weight.
  • Only if listed on the consent screen: blood-oxygen (SpO2) averages ("spo2"), workout summaries ("workout"), or heart-health data ("heart_health").

We don't request your Oura email address, and we don't collect or store location information from Oura.

How we use it. Only to run the study you agreed to join (including research on how sleep, health, and daily rhythms relate to relationships), to show you your own summaries, and to keep the connection working and secure. We analyze study data in de-identified, aggregated form. We never try to re-identify you from it, and we never combine it with other data to identify you or to build profiles beyond the study you consented to.

What we never do with Oura data. We never sell it. We never use it, or the fact that you connected Oura, for advertising, marketing, or promoting other Hatch Labs products, and we never use it to contact you about anything other than the study. We never use it to train, fine-tune, or feed artificial-intelligence or machine-learning models. We never use it to make credit, insurance, or employment decisions about you. We never charge you for connecting Oura.

Who can see it. Hatch Labs staff and contractors who run the study, limited to what their role requires, and our service providers (Supabase for database hosting and Cloudflare for hosting and security), which process it only on our behalf. Your partner, or anyone else, will never see your Oura data unless you expressly choose to share it with them. We don't share identifiable Oura data with scientists or other third parties. We share Oura-derived data with outside researchers, or publish findings based on it, only in aggregated form that can't identify you and only as our agreement with Oura permits, including any approval Oura requires. If we ever want to share your Oura data in any other way, we'll ask for your separate consent first. We may disclose information if the law requires it.

How long we keep it. Only as long as it's strictly needed for the study you consented to. It is not kept for future studies or for other Hatch Labs products.

  • If you disconnect Oura or withdraw consent, we stop collecting and using your Oura data right away.
  • If you ask us to delete it, or delete your application or account, we delete all of your Oura data within 72 hours.
  • When the study ends, or you leave it, we delete your Oura data.

How to disconnect. Use Disconnect next to Oura on our site; remove I Hereby Commit from the connected or authorized apps in your Oura account; or email contact@hatchlabs.com. Disconnecting won't affect your Oura membership or your use of Oura's products.

Security. Oura data is encrypted in transit and at rest. Only authorized people can reach it, using multi-factor authentication. We maintain an incident-response process and will notify affected people and partners as the law and our agreements require.

Oura's own policy. Oura handles your data on its side under its own privacy policy and terms. I Hereby Commit is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by Oura.

Apple Health dataOPTIONAL

This section applies only if you choose to connect Apple Health in our iPhone app. Apple Health data stays on your iPhone, and apps can reach it only through Apple's HealthKit, so it can't be connected from our website. Connecting is optional, is offered after you register, and isn't required to apply or to stay in the study. Apple Health data is health data, so we treat it with the same extra care as Oura data.

What we read. We ask only for the data types listed on our consent screen, and you can switch each one on or off on Apple's own permission screen. We may ask to read:

  • Sleep: time asleep and in bed, and sleep stages.
  • Heart rate: heart-rate readings over time and resting heart rate.
  • Heart rate variability (HRV).
  • Activity: steps, active energy (calories burned through movement), and exercise minutes.

We don't ask for cycle tracking, sexual activity, pregnancy, or any other reproductive or sexual health data. We don't read health records, medications, your Medical ID, workout routes or other location data, or any Apple Health data type not listed above. If we ever want to add a data type, we'll update this policy first and you'll be asked again on Apple's permission screen.

Read-only. We only read. Our app never writes, changes, or deletes anything in Apple Health.

How we use it. Only to show you your own summaries in the app and, if you separately agree to take part in a research study that uses it, for that study. Research use always needs your separate informed consent, which explains the study's nature, purpose, and duration; its procedures, risks, and benefits; how your data is kept confidential and whether it's shared with anyone; who to contact with questions; and how to withdraw. Any such study must be approved and overseen by an independent ethics review board (an IRB). Without that consent, your Apple Health data is not used for research. Research analyses use de-identified, aggregated data, and we never try to re-identify you from it.

What we never do with Apple Health data. We never sell it, including to advertising platforms, data brokers, or information resellers. We never use it, or the fact that you connected Apple Health, for advertising, marketing, or promoting other Hatch Labs products. We never use it for data mining or to build a profile of you, and never to make credit, insurance, or employment decisions about you. We never use it to train, fine-tune, or feed artificial-intelligence or machine-learning models. We never charge you for connecting Apple Health.

Who can see it. Hatch Labs staff and contractors who run the study, limited to what their role requires, and our service providers (Supabase for database hosting and Cloudflare for hosting and security), which process it only on our behalf and may not use it for themselves. Your partner, or anyone else, will never see your Apple Health data unless you expressly choose to share it with them. We don't disclose Apple Health data to any other third party without your express permission, and even with it, only to a party that provides a health or fitness service to you or for health research you've consented to. We don't send your Apple Health data to Apple. We may disclose information if the law requires it.

Where it's stored. In our access-restricted database in the United States, encrypted in transit and at rest. We never store Apple Health data in iCloud: our app doesn't save it to iCloud, iCloud Drive, or iCloud backups.

How long we keep it. Only as long as it's strictly needed for the features you use or the study you consented to. It is not kept for future studies or for other Hatch Labs products.

  • If you turn off our access in your iPhone's settings, we stop receiving new Apple Health data right away. iOS doesn't tell apps when access is turned off, so this doesn't delete what we already received; use Disconnect or email us for that.
  • If you disconnect Apple Health in our app, ask us to delete it, or delete your application or account, we delete all of your Apple Health data within 72 hours.
  • When the study ends, or you leave it, we delete your Apple Health data.

How to disconnect. Use Disconnect next to Apple Health in our iPhone app. Or, on your iPhone, go to Settings → Health → Data Access & Devices → I Hereby Commit and tap Turn Off All (on some iOS versions it's Settings → Apps → Health → Data Access & Devices). Or email contact@hatchlabs.com. Deleting our app from your iPhone stops it reading Apple Health but doesn't delete what we already received. Disconnecting never changes or deletes the data in Apple Health on your iPhone.

Apple's own policy. Your Apple Health data on your iPhone is handled by Apple under its own privacy policy. I Hereby Commit is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by Apple. Apple, Apple Health, HealthKit, and iPhone are trademarks of Apple Inc.
